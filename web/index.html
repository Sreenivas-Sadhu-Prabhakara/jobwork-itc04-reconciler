The rule this applies

Under section 143 of the CGST Act, 2017 a principal may send inputs or capital goods to a job worker without paying tax, provided the goods are brought back — or supplied from the job worker's premises — within one year for inputs and three years for capital goods. If they are not, section 143(3) and 143(4) treat them as having been supplied to the job worker on the day they were sent out, and tax falls due from that day with interest.

Rule 45 of the CGST Rules, 2017 requires the goods to move under a delivery challan and the challans of a period to be reported in Form ITC-04. This tool keeps that register, works out each challan's deadline, pro-rates the taxable value of whatever is still out, and groups a period by job worker so the figures can be keyed into the return.

Assumptions, and what this tool does not do

Deadlines are calendar arithmetic from the challan date: one year for inputs, three for capital goods. A challan dated 29 February falls due on 1 March of the target year, that date not existing there. The deadline date itself is still within time; the day after it is not.

The three-year period does not apply to moulds and dies, jigs and fixtures, or tools sent out to a job worker — see the proviso to section 143(1). This tool applies three years to everything marked as capital goods, so keep those four kinds of item out of the register.

Outstanding quantity is the quantity sent less every quantity recorded back against that challan, whenever it came back. Returns may be partial and may arrive in instalments. If more is recorded back than went out, the outstanding quantity is held at zero and the excess is flagged rather than quietly cancelling a real exposure elsewhere.

Deemed supply value is the challan's taxable value pro-rated by quantity: value × outstanding ÷ sent. It is the value tax would fall on, not the tax. The tool does not apply a rate, work out interest, or decide the place of supply.

Filing frequency. Rule 45(3), as substituted with effect from 1 October 2021, puts a principal whose annual aggregate turnover in the preceding financial year is above ₹5 crore on a half-yearly period (April–September and October–March) and one at or below ₹5 crore on a period of one financial year. Either is due on the 25th of the month after the period ends, unless extended. Before 1 October 2021 every principal filed quarterly, which is why a quarterly period is still offered here. Confirm the current frequency, threshold and due date with the CGST Rules or your tax adviser — they are editable under Settings.

GSTINs are checked for the 15-character shape only. The check digit is not recomputed, so a GSTIN that passes may still not exist.

Quantities are added only within a unit of measure. Where a total would mix units the tool says so rather than implying that kilograms and pieces add up.

This prepares the figures for a filing; it is not a filing. There is no connection to the GST portal, and no advice here is a substitute for your own tax adviser. Check every figure against your challans before you file.
